    What the data says

    These patterns come from 48,994 open-play shots in the training matches. Each one has an example you can load onto the board.

    Distance does most of the work

    56% of shots from inside 5 yards were scored, against 10% from 15 to 20 yards and under 2.5% beyond 25.

    Every body in the way counts

    With nobody between the ball and the goal, 44% went in. With one player in the way it was 13%, with two 6%.

    A header is worse than it looks

    Inside 12 yards, 31% of shots with the foot were scored but only 13% of headers. Over all distances they look equal, because headers are taken closer in.

    Angle matters as much as distance

    Goals were scored with a median of 32° of goal to aim at. Shots that missed had 19°.

    A keeper off the line is a gamble

    When a goal went in, the keeper was a median 3.6 yards from goal, against 3.0 for saves and misses. Rushing out narrows the angle but leaves the goal behind.

    About the model

    The model learned from 61,003 open-play shots in 2,616 men's matches from StatsBomb's open data: La Liga, the Premier League, Serie A, Ligue 1, World Cups, Euros and more. Penalties and direct free kicks are left out, because they are different kinds of chance.

    It was tested on 524 matches it never saw. Across those matches it predicted within 1.5% of the goals actually scored, so its numbers can be added up for a player or a team.

    Both models on this page run in your browser. Boosted trees (CatBoost) is the one chosen in the study. Logistic regression is simpler and almost as accurate; its xG changes more smoothly as you drag.

    Things it can't see

    • Who is shooting. A chance is priced for an average finisher, which is why players like Messi score far more than their xG.
    • How the shot is struck: power, placement and technique come after the chance, so they belong to a different measure.
    • Teammates, the speed of the attack, or which foot is stronger.
